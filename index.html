MOUTHPIECE

Type anything. Hear it built from real clips of real people actually saying it: whole words where they exist, syllables and single sounds cut out of other words where they don't. Every fragment says where it came from.

Not TTS.

Make it say something

Toward flow, phrases spoken together in one recording are reused whole. Toward fragments, words that exist whole are cut apart anyway, down to single sounds at the far end.

    01

    How a word gets built

    Mouthpiece tries the most natural option first and falls back one tier at a time. The colours match the machine above.

    1. 01

      whole word

      The exact word was spoken somewhere in the corpus. Use that clip directly. Most natural, when it's available.

    2. 02

      phrase reuse

      Consecutive words were spoken together in one recording. Reuse the whole span as one clip: real co-articulation, no seam.

    3. 03

      syllable stitch

      Look the word up in CMUdict, split it into syllables, and splice them from other words, keeping runs that were spoken together where they exist.

    4. 04

      phoneme fallback

      Fall all the way down to individual phones. Rougher, but it makes almost any word constructible. Whichever of 03 and 04 needs fewer splices wins.

    Sounded out is this page's own addition: a word CMUdict can't place at all gets pieced together from dictionary fragments and letter sounds. The engine leaves a gap instead.

    Every fragment is attributable

    Each render writes a .manifest.json next to the WAV: which recording every word came from, who said it, when, and where in the file. Missing words become 400 ms gaps listed as found: false. The machine above gives you the same kind of manifest.

    the recordings

      All recordings are public domain, from archive.org and the Obama White House archive. LibriVox entries name the book's author; the reader is a LibriVox volunteer.

      04

      Run it on your own corpus

      Python 3.11, WhisperX for transcription and word alignment, SQLite for the index. A GPU is strongly recommended for transcription.

      1. Install

        python3.11 -m venv .venv && source .venv/bin/activate
pip install torch torchaudio   # CUDA build recommended
pip install -e .

      2. Ingest a recording

        mouthpiece ingest corpus/myspeech.wav --metadata corpus/myspeech.json

        Normalizes to 16 kHz mono, transcribes, aligns word timestamps, writes the index. Metadata (speaker, date, event, source URL, license) is optional.

      3. Check coverage, then render

        mouthpiece coverage "we must act now"
mouthpiece "we must act now" -o renders/out.wav

        Writes a WAV that drops straight into Reaper, plus its manifest.
